## PRICING & PAYMENT

**Q: Do you offer payment plans?**
A: Yes, for select properties we offer structured payment plans of up to 24 months for a 30% deposit, with the balance payable on completion or in agreed installments. Terms vary by property and developer.

**Q: Are prices negotiable?**
A: Most listed prices have some room for negotiation, particularly for properties that have been on the market for over 60 days. Our agents can advise on realistic offer ranges during a viewing.

**Q: What additional costs should I budget for?**
A: Beyond the purchase price, buyers should budget for legal fees (typically 1-2% of purchase price), stamp duty (4% for properties within municipalities, 2% outside), valuation fees, land search fees, and agency commission (typically 2-3%, paid by the seller in most transactions). For land, also budget for a survey/beacon verification if one hasn't been done recently.

**Q: Do you assist with mortgage financing?**
A: Yes, we work with several partner banks and can facilitate introductions for mortgage pre-approval, including options under Kenya's affordable housing financing schemes for qualifying buyers. Financing terms depend on the buyer's bank and eligibility.

**Q: What ongoing costs come with owning an apartment or a home in a gated community?**
A: Apartments and gated-community homes typically carry a monthly service charge covering security, common-area maintenance, water, and refuse collection — this is separate from the purchase price and varies by development. All properties also carry annual land rates (for land) or land rent (for leasehold land), payable to the relevant county government or the Ministry of Lands.

## OFF-PLAN PURCHASES

**Q: Is it safe to buy off-plan?**
A: We only list off-plan projects from developers we've vetted for financial standing and delivery track record, and we require deposits to be held in an escrow account rather than paid directly to the developer, so your funds are protected if the project is delayed or doesn't proceed.

**Q: What happens if an off-plan project is delayed?**
A: Reputable developments include a sale agreement clause specifying the expected completion date and the remedy for delays (commonly a grace period followed by compensation or a refund option). We review this clause with you before you commit to a booking.

## DIASPORA CLIENTS

**Q: I live abroad — can I buy a property without traveling to Kenya?**
A: Yes. Most of our diaspora clients complete the entire process remotely: viewings are done via video call, and you sign a Power of Attorney (POA), notarized at a Kenyan embassy/consulate or a notary public in your country of residence, authorizing a trusted representative (often our partner advocate) to sign documents and register the transfer on your behalf.

**Q: What documents do I need as a diaspora buyer?**
A: A copy of your passport or national ID, KRA PIN (or assistance obtaining one, which we can facilitate), the notarized Power of Attorney, and proof of funds for the transaction. Payments are typically made via bank wire transfer directly into the designated escrow or advocate's client account.

**Q: Can I own land in Kenya as a non-citizen?**
A: Kenyan law reserves freehold land ownership for citizens. Non-citizens (including foreign nationals and, in some cases, dual citizens) can hold land on leasehold terms of up to 99 years. This restriction applies to land itself — sectional-title apartments and units are commonly structured to make ownership straightforward for diaspora and foreign buyers, and our agents can walk you through which of our listings are freehold vs. leasehold.

## LEGAL & PROCESS

**Q: What is the typical buying process?**
A: 1) Property viewing and offer, 2) offer acceptance and deposit payment, 3) legal due diligence and title search, 4) sale agreement signing, 5) balance payment and transfer of title. The full process typically takes 6-10 weeks depending on financing, and is longer for off-plan purchases.

**Q: Do you verify property titles before listing?**
A: Yes — every property is checked against the official land register via Ardhisasa (Kenya's national land information platform) to confirm registered ownership and flag any existing charges, caveats, or restrictions, in addition to our own due diligence. Buyers are still encouraged to have their own advocate conduct an independent search before signing.

**Q: Do I need a lawyer to buy property?**
A: Yes — property transfers in Kenya must be handled by a Law Society of Kenya (LSK)-licensed advocate, who conducts the official search, prepares the sale agreement and transfer documents, accounts for stamp duty, and lodges the transfer with the Lands Registry. We can recommend an independent advocate, or you're welcome to use your own.

**Q: Can foreign nationals purchase property through Nyumba Prime?**
A: Yes, with one legal distinction to be aware of: foreign nationals cannot hold freehold land in Kenya, only leasehold (up to 99 years). Sectional-title apartments, like most of our Nairobi listings, are commonly available to foreign and diaspora buyers without this restriction applying to the land itself. We recommend consulting a property lawyer for guidance specific to your situation.
