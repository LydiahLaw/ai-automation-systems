## TEXAS AUTO INSURANCE

**Q: What is the minimum auto insurance required in Texas?**
A: Texas requires liability coverage of at least 30/60/25: $30,000 for injuries per person, $60,000 for injuries per accident, and $25,000 for property damage. These minimums can be too low after a serious accident or when a newer vehicle is totaled, so many drivers choose higher limits.

**Q: What is liability coverage?**
A: Liability coverage pays for injuries and property damage you cause to other people in an accident. It does not pay for your own car or your own injuries.

**Q: What is Personal Injury Protection (PIP)?**
A: PIP pays medical bills and some other costs, such as lost wages, for you and your passengers regardless of who caused the accident. In Texas, auto policies include PIP unless you reject it in writing.

**Q: What is uninsured and underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage?**
A: UM/UIM coverage helps pay for your injuries and, depending on the policy, vehicle damage when the other driver has no insurance or not enough. Texas insurers must offer it, and you can decline it only in writing.

**Q: What is the difference between collision and comprehensive?**
A: Collision covers repairing or replacing your car after an accident. Comprehensive covers non-collision events such as theft, fire, flood, vandalism, and hail. Both have a deductible. If you have a car loan or lease, the lender usually requires both.

**Q: What happens if I drive without insurance in Texas?**
A: Driving without insurance is a misdemeanor. Fines typically start around $175 to $350 for a first offense and increase for repeat offenses, with possible surcharges, vehicle impoundment, and license consequences. Texas also verifies insurance electronically through the TexasSure system. A licensed agent can help you get covered quickly.

**Q: What is an SR-22?**
A: An SR-22 is not insurance. It is a certificate your insurance company files with the state to prove you carry the required liability coverage. It is typically required after events such as a DWI conviction or driving without insurance, and usually must be kept for two years without a lapse. Not every carrier files SR-22s, and our agents can find ones that do.

**Q: What if I was turned down for auto insurance?**
A: If at least two companies have declined you, you may qualify for the Texas Automobile Insurance Plan Association (TAIPA). It offers liability, PIP, and UM coverage only, costs more than standard coverage, and rates can drop after a period with no tickets or accidents. It is applied for through an agent.

**Q: What should I do after a car accident?**
A: Check for injuries and call 911 if anyone is hurt. Move to a safe place if you can, exchange information with the other driver, take photos, and report the accident to your insurance company promptly. Our agents can help you understand next steps, but your carrier handles the claim itself.

## HOMEOWNERS AND RENTERS INSURANCE

**Q: What does homeowners insurance usually cover?**
A: Most policies cover fire and lightning, sudden water or smoke damage, theft, vandalism, explosion, and windstorm and hail (except in coastal areas, see below). Coverage typically includes the home, other structures, personal belongings, liability, and additional living expenses if you cannot live in your home after a covered loss.

**Q: What does homeowners insurance usually not cover?**
A: Most policies do not cover flood, continuous leaks, termites and rodents, wear and tear, or earthquakes. Wind and hail damage to trees and shrubs is generally excluded. Most policies also do not cover damage or injuries related to short-term rentals. Always check your policy, and ask your agent about gaps.

**Q: What is the difference between replacement cost and actual cash value?**
A: Replacement cost pays what it costs to repair or replace at today's prices. Actual cash value subtracts depreciation, so you receive less. For example, with an older roof, actual cash value can leave you paying a large part of the repair yourself. Replacement cost coverage is generally recommended.

**Q: How much dwelling coverage do I need?**
A: Most companies require insuring your home for at least 80 percent of its replacement cost, and some require 100 percent. Replacement cost is what it would cost to rebuild, not the market value or purchase price.

**Q: How are belongings and living expenses covered?**
A: Personal property is usually a percentage of the dwelling limit, often around 20 percent. Additional living expenses are commonly limited to a percentage of the dwelling limit, often 10 to 20 percent. Your declarations page shows your exact limits.

**Q: Why is my hail or wind deductible different?**
A: Deductibles can differ by type of loss, and wind and hail deductibles are often a percentage of your dwelling coverage instead of a flat dollar amount. Check your policy declarations page for your exact deductible or ask an agent.

**Q: What does renters insurance cover?**
A: Renters insurance covers your belongings, personal liability, and additional living expenses after a covered loss. It does not cover the building itself, which is the landlord's responsibility. It is typically affordable and many landlords require it.

**Q: Can my insurance company cancel my homeowners policy?**
A: Texas law limits cancellation. Your insurer must give advance notice before canceling, and during the first 60 days of a new policy, cancellation is allowed only for specific reasons, such as an undisclosed risk. Unearned premium must be refunded after cancellation. Nonrenewal also requires advance written notice, and you can ask the insurer for the reason. An agent can explain your options if you receive a notice.

**Q: What if I cannot find homeowners coverage?**
A: If at least two companies have declined you, you may qualify for the Texas FAIR Plan Association. Our agents can help with the application.

## COASTAL WINDSTORM (TWIA)

**Q: What is TWIA?**
A: The Texas Windstorm Insurance Association (TWIA) provides windstorm and hail coverage to coastal property owners who cannot get it in the regular market. It covers wind and hail only, not flood.

**Q: Which areas are eligible for TWIA?**
A: Properties in the designated coastal area: Aransas, Brazoria, Calhoun, Cameron, Chambers, Galveston, Jefferson, Kenedy, Kleberg, Matagorda, Nueces, Refugio, San Patricio, and Willacy counties, plus parts of Harris County east of Highway 146. Our Corpus Christi branch (Nueces County) handles many of these requests.

**Q: What are the requirements to get a TWIA policy?**
A: The property must be in the designated area, you must have been denied wind and hail coverage by an authorized insurer, and the property must be certified as built to applicable windstorm building codes (a WPI-8 certification, with limited exceptions) and be in good repair. Flood insurance is also required for certain properties in high-risk flood zones built or substantially altered on or after September 1, 2009. TWIA is applied for through a licensed agent.

**Q: Can I buy TWIA coverage when a hurricane is approaching?**
A: No. TWIA does not sell new policies once a hurricane is in the Gulf of Mexico, so coastal homeowners should arrange coverage well before hurricane season.

**Q: How long do I have to file a TWIA claim?**
A: TWIA claims must be filed within one year of the damage. Report damage as soon as possible after a storm.

## FLOOD INSURANCE

**Q: Does homeowners insurance cover flooding?**
A: No. Standard homeowners and renters policies do not cover flood damage. Flood coverage is a separate policy.

**Q: Where does flood insurance come from?**
A: Flood insurance is available through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) and from some private insurers. NFIP coverage is generally available up to $250,000 for the building and $100,000 for contents. Private policies may offer higher limits.

**Q: Is there a waiting period for flood insurance?**
A: Yes. Flood policies typically have a 30-day waiting period before coverage starts, so it is important to buy before a storm threatens. If your lender requires flood insurance, ask your agent about the timing.

**Q: Do I need flood insurance if I do not live in a flood zone?**
A: Flooding can happen anywhere in Texas, and many flood claims come from areas outside high-risk zones. Our agents can explain your risk and options.

## FILING AND TRACKING A CLAIM

**Q: How do I file a claim?**
A: Contact your insurance carrier directly using the claims number on your policy or insurance card. Report the loss promptly, take photos or video of damage before repairs, make only temporary repairs to prevent further damage (such as tarping a roof), and keep all receipts. Our agents can help you understand the process, but the carrier handles the claim.

**Q: How quickly must my insurance company respond to my claim?**
A: Under the Texas Prompt Payment of Claims law, an insurer generally must acknowledge your claim within 15 days, accept or reject it within 15 business days after receiving all the information it requested (with extensions possible, up to 45 days in some cases), and pay within 5 business days after agreeing to pay. TWIA has its own timelines. These are general timelines, and exceptions can apply.

**Q: What should I have ready for the adjuster?**
A: Photos or video of the damage, a list of damaged items, receipts for temporary repairs, your policy number, and your contact information. Be present for the inspection if possible.

**Q: What if I am unhappy with how my claim was handled?**
A: Start by speaking with your carrier and asking for the reason for their decision in writing. You can also contact the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) Help Line at 800-252-3439, Monday to Friday, 8:00 AM to 5:00 PM Central Time, or file a complaint online through TDI's website. TDI can also help verify whether an adjuster or public adjuster is licensed. For legal advice about a dispute, consult an attorney.

**Q: Are there ways to lower my premium?**

**Q: How can I pay my premium?**
A: Payment options depend on your carrier and can include monthly, quarterly, or annual payments. Your agent can explain what your carrier accepts.
